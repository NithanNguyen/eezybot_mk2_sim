## 1. Model construction

`build_model.py` writes three MuJoCo models from the original STL files and `params.py`:

| Model | Content |
|---|---|
| `model/mk2.xml` | Arm + real gripper, no pen (pick-and-place, default) |
| `model/mk2_pen.xml` | Adds a Ø10 pen held vertically between the fingers and a 1 mm thick A4 sheet |
| `model/mk2_sort.xml` | Colour-sorting scene (see [sorting-task.md](sorting-task.md)), objects from `sort_scene.py` |

- **Arm:** built from **13 original STL files** with **3 closed parallelogram loops** (`equality/connect`). Every part is placed from shaft-hole centres and mating faces measured on the STL (`tools/holes.py`).
- **Real gripper:** a custom design with no CAD file. It is rebuilt from caliper measurements and profiles extracted from photos (`gripper.py`, `gripper_shape.py`).
  - Mechanism: two parallelograms, **translating** fingers, two 1:1 gears, SG90 shaft coaxial with the left gear.
  - The gripper replaces the whole wrist block `009` and the original clamp `014–018`.
  - Profiles are generated by `tools/gripper_from_photos.py` (similarity fit after Umeyama 1991, contour simplification after Douglas–Peucker 1973); polygons are triangulated by ear clipping (Eberly). See [references.md](references.md).
- **Pen (`--pen` only):** Ø10 pen held vertically between the fingers, pen axis 27 mm back from the fingertip, tip 90 mm below the grip point. A **1 mm wide ink trace** is recorded only when MuJoCo reports tip–paper contact and is shown in the viewer.
- **Servos:** MG996R (base, shoulder, elbow) and SG90 (gripper), modelled as torque-saturated P controllers with a DC-motor torque–speed slope (set through `damping`). Servo boxes use datasheet dimensions.
- **Masses:** computed from STL volumes (PLA 1.24 g/cm³, 100 % infill by default; `FILL_FACTOR`, `MEASURED_MASS_G` in `params.py`). Report written to `model/mass_report.txt`.
- **Assembly check:** `tools/check_assembly.py` verifies that no parts interpenetrate within the joint limits and that the gripper does not self-collide over 0–90° ([installation.md §5](installation.md#5-assembly-check-recommended-run-once)).

All parameters live in `params.py`, each tagged with its source: `[DO-THAT]` measured on the real robot, `[ANH]` from photos,
`[DO-STL]` computed from STL, `[GIA-DINH]` assumed. See [calibration-and-limitations.md](calibration-and-limitations.md).

## 2. Kinematics

`mk2_kinematics.py` is a closed-form FK/IK library written in plain Python/NumPy, independent of MuJoCo, so the same formulas can be ported to C/C++.

Joint variables (rad):
- `th1`: base joint angle about the vertical axis (0 = +x).
- `th2`: **absolute** angle of the main arm O→E from horizontal.
- `phi`: **absolute** angle of the horizontal arm E→W from horizontal.

Because of the parallelogram linkage, the elbow servo (through crank `003_varm`) sets `phi` directly, not the relative DH angle.
This is the "mapping layer" of the project proposal; the standard DH angle is `q3_DH = phi − th2`.
The tool keeps a fixed orientation (second and third parallelograms), so its position depends only on (`th1`, `th2`, `phi`).

Tool points, selected with `set_tool()` (offsets `DX`, `DZ` relative to the wrist centre W, in mm):

| Tool | `set_tool()` | (DX, DZ) |
|---|---|---|
| Gripper TCP (default): midpoint of the fingertip pads when closed, 5 mm back from the tip | `"gripper"` | (132.69, 0.50) |
| Pen tip (`--pen` models only) | `"pen"` | (110.59, −89.50) |
| Cap centre when gripped (sorting task) | `"cap"` | (110.00, 0.50) |

The tool is offset laterally from the arm plane by `DY` = 0.48 mm (`GRIP_CENTER_Y`, assumed); FK and IK both include it.
`within_limits()` checks θ1, θ2, φ, φ − θ2, the servo command ranges `CTRL_*_DEG` and the servo travel; IK raises `IKError`
for any point that would require a command outside these ranges instead of silently clipping it.

Verification (`check_kinematics.py`, [simulation-guide.md §2](simulation-guide.md#2-kinematics-verification)): FK(IK(p)) round trip
on 5000 points, closed-loop MuJoCo vs analytic FK on 40 poses, gravity sag under finite-gain servos, and the feasible drawing size table.

## 3. Trajectories (M0, M2)

`trajectories.py` implements the test figure and the two trajectory methods from the project proposal:

- **Figure:** a 5-point star (10 segments) followed by a circle of radius R, top star tip pointing +x (away from the robot). Path length L = 13.55 R.
- **M0:** linear interpolation with constant tangential speed v = L/T.
- **M2:** double-S profile limiting v, a, j along arc length, stopping at every vertex. **Ruckig** is used as the time-optimal reference (Berscheid & Kröger, RSS 2021).

Both are sampled every `CMD_PERIOD` (20 ms) and passed through IK at every sample; each command is held for 20 ms in MuJoCo (`sim_common.py`).
For the comparison, M0 uses the same duration T as M2.

A custom double-S generator intended for the ESP32 can replace `m2_path` in `trajectories.py` and be compared against Ruckig.

## 4. Drawing metrics

Measured on the **ink trace** (only where the tip touched the paper), as one would measure on real paper:

- **Overshoot:** measured against the **intersection of the two drawn edges, extended**. A **negative** value means the stroke **cuts the corner** and does not reach the intersection.
- **Vertex error:** shortest distance from the stroke to that intersection. It captures both overshoot and corner cutting.
- **Roundness:** D_max − D_min over the four directions 0°, 45°, 90°, 135°.
- **Straightness error:** largest gap over the 15–85 % portion of each edge.
- **Ink coverage %:** share of the drawing time with the tip on the paper. Below 100 % means a broken stroke.

## 5. Findings from the drawing simulation

To be verified on hardware:
- M0 **cuts corners** by about 0.8 mm rather than overshooting, so the "overshoot" metric in the proposal can be negative for M0. Measuring "vertex error" as well is advisable.
- The pen is clamped rigidly and the tip command is exactly at the paper surface. Gravity sag ([simulation-guide.md §2](simulation-guide.md#2-kinematics-verification), line [3]) presses the tip into the paper with a mean force of about 3.1 N.
- "Max straightness error" is about 1.2 mm for both M0 and M2. Its cause has not been analysed.
- *Data from the old clamp, not yet re-run with the real gripper:* with μ ≈ 0 the normal force drops to ~1.3 N and the mean M2 overshoot changes from −0.38 to 0.00 mm; with friction or gravity disabled, the straightness error remains. Results are sensitive to tip–paper friction (`PEN_TIP_FRICTION`, assumed 0.2), which should be measured on the real pen.

## 6. Porting to ESP32

`mk2_kinematics.py` only uses `math`, so its formulas can be copied to C/C++ (`float`). Functions to port:

- `ik()`: atan2, acos, upper elbow solution, **lateral offset compensation `DY`** = 0.48 mm (`th1 = atan2(y, x) − atan2(DY, r)`).
- Tool constants `DX`, `DZ` (table in section 2), matching `set_tool()`.
- `to_servo()`: mapping layer with offsets, rotation signs and the 2:1 base gearbox.
- `within_limits()`: check θ1, θ2, φ, φ − θ2, `CTRL_*_DEG` and servo travel before sending a command.

To compare float (ESP32) with double (PC) as the proposal requires, compare the `servo*_deg` columns of `results/log_M2.csv`
with the ESP32 UART log for the same trajectory.
